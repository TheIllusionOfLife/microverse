01 / 11The origin

The original vision

In Rick and Morty, the Microverse Battery is a whole miniature universe whose inhabitants unknowingly power a single car. This project borrows the metaphor and flips it toward something kind: build a small living world of AI agents that runs autonomously for weeks on local hardware, and harvest the best of what they make.

Local-first, forever

Every “thought” is a local Ollama call against one small Gemma model. No network, no API bill, no per-token meter. The bottleneck is your laptop, not your budget.

A society, not a chatbot

Inhabitants live in-world: they create, study, talk, and build shared work. A single out-of-world Harvester ferries the best artifacts out to you.

Honest by construction

Every event lands in a local SQLite log with full provenance. Nothing is hidden; the documented limits are documented honestly.

02 / 11The build

What we built

A single-process tick loop where one model drives every mind, durability is a SQLite WAL, and memory is split into what happened (episodic) and what is known (semantic lore).

One model, no router

Every agent.think() goes through a single gemma4:26b call. No fallback, no secret second brain. A reviewer can verify the invariant by eye.

WAL is the truth

SQLite write-ahead log is the durability boundary; a hard kill loses only the in-flight tick. Cold snapshots are backups, never the recovery path.

Scenes

The headline v1.0 idea: 3-turn micro-scenes where turn 2 reads turn 1 and turn 3 reads both — a structured unit of collaborative artifact instead of solo monologue.

Measured, not asserted

A measurement-only embedding model (nomic-embed-text) scores whether scene turns truly depend on each other — observability that never touches the agent loop.

03 / 11The path

The journey

v0.1 One mind. A single Artisan crafting in a loop — proof the local tick loop and WAL durability hold.

v0.4 Scenes & embeddings. Multi-turn collaboration and the semantic-dependence measurement that proves it.
v1.0.0-rc1 The long run. A 5.5-day soak on one laptop, eight acceptance gates measured, the result reported honestly.

06 / 11What it wrote

07 / 11The shortfalls

What failed — and why it’s good news

Gate 1: lexical, not real, mismatch

Agents engage by paraphrase and idea-extension, not by literally naming each other. The surface-form regex under-fired (0.073) while the semantic gate passed strongly. We reclassified the lexical proxy as observational and promoted the embedding gate to authoritative — exactly the path we pre-committed to.

Gate 3: a lever that never pulled

The verb-diversity nudge stayed at zero substitutions all soak. Root cause: it watched scene contributions (94% of activity) and so could never match a free single-tick action. Found, understood, and fixed — it can fire on the next run.

The snapshot “stall”

A cold-backup checkpoint hit a disk I/O error 9,106 times and kept retrying, which made out-of-process monitors think the world had stalled. It never had (51.5s max gap). A new circuit breaker trips after 5 failures and moves on. The simulation core was never the problem.

Gates 4 & 7: close, and explainable

Fold rate missed <1% but improved 28×; capacity violations track the snapshot windows. Both are sharp, bounded follow-ups for v1.1, not signs of an unsound core.

The discipline: the failures are reported as the system actually ran, not as patched after the fact. The fixes target the next soak. That’s why this is rc1, not a victory-lap v1.0.0.
08 / 11The distance

The gap between vision and today

The dream was “a living society running for weeks, in rich dialogue.” Here’s the honest distance left.

Weeks → 5.5 days

We proved multi-day, not multi-week. The only thing that stopped us was a backup bug, now fixed — the engine itself never tired.

Dialogue → dependence

Turns demonstrably read each other (semantic), but don’t yet visibly cross-reference (lexical). The conversation is real; it just doesn’t look like quoting.

Variety → monoculture

One verb still dominates each agent. The model-level attractor is real; our structural counter-pressure exists but hadn’t fired yet.

A world → two residents

The default cast is two. A richer society needs more inhabitants — which also unlocks deeper, multi-peer scenes.

09 / 11The plan

How we close it

Re-soak with the fixes

Snapshot breaker + revived diversity lever in place, push past 5.5 days toward the full “weeks” claim.

Grow the roster

More residents (and roles) unlock 3-distinct-peer scenes, richer dynamics, and a real test of emergent culture.

Better peer-reference signal

Replace the brittle lexical regex with a semantic-aware measure so “they’re engaging” is captured the way it actually happens.

Break the verb monoculture

Now that the lever can fire, measure how much genuine variety it buys — and whether digest-style artifacts help.

Cross-family curiosity

Later, swap the single model for a different family and ask whether a different mind makes a different society.

Stress & scale

Decouple snapshots from the write path; run several microverses in parallel on one machine.

10 / 11The leap

From workshop to civilization

Today the agents live a single looping good day — always spring, always the workshop, always beginning. The original vision was bigger: a society that remembers, accumulates, and changes — a history of its own. What v1.0 built is a collaboration engine. A civilization needs three things collaboration alone never produces — durable identity, scarcity, and accumulation — so this is the leap that gets them there.

Why nothing accretes yet: the soak threw away work as fast as it made it (1,845 recycles), memory is a sliding window (no durable self), the population never grew past two, nothing was ever scarce, and the longest shared intention lasted three turns. The verb monoculture and shallow cross-reference aren’t separate bugs — they’re symptoms of that missing substrate.

1 · A self that persists

the unlock

Give each agent a durable record — traits, beliefs, and a relationship ledger of who helped whom and who owes whom. “Cy repaid the favor in the drought” becomes a fact the world keeps. Culture can’t stick to amnesia.

2 · Scarcity & an economy

the engine

Make materials finite and agents unequally skilled, so trade becomes rational and the Trader becomes a real market. Scarcity is what forces specialization, property, and the first institutions.

3 · An accumulation ratchet

the definition

Stop recycling. Build durable, append-only structures that grow — a village ledger, constructed lore, a technique tree. Each generation builds on the last. That’s the line between a society and a sandbox.

4 · Population & turnover

variation

Let newcomers arrive with different priors and let prosperity drive who stays. Turnover lets norms be inherited and mutated — the actual mechanism of cultural evolution, and the cure for monoculture.

5 · Goals & governance

institutions

Add projects that span hundreds of ticks and a thin propose-assent-record loop, so decisions become norms with teeth. Institutions are just remembered agreements that constrain what comes next.

The honest constraint

scaffold, not genius

One small local model can’t plan a civilization. So the structure carries the long arc — scarcity, ledgers, lasting goals — and the model just makes good local choices inside it. That’s how real institutions work too.

The reframe: v1.0 optimizes for artifacts — the best scrolls. The leap is to optimize for accumulated structure and divergence. The interesting object was never the scroll; it’s the society that made it and how it changed. Harvest the history, not just the output. (design recorded in ADR 0007 — planning, not yet built.)
